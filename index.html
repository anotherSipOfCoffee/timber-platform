A timber building system, kept deliberately simple.

A small family of timber parts, cut by CNC, that can become many different small buildings — without turning every project into a custom exercise.

System 0.1 · conceptual / pre-engineering · no structural claims yet

The architecture is allowed to change. The interfaces underneath it stay calm.
01 / the idea

Standardise the quiet parts.

The aim is not to make every building look the same. It is to make the things behind the look behave in a predictable way.

The current research is built around a frame-and-cartridge approach: a sparse timber frame carries the main structure, while floor, wall and roof elements can change independently. That separation is useful for repair, façade choices, services and future suppliers.

The test is bigger than one prototype. The system only becomes convincing when many valid buildings can be generated without a matching explosion in unique parts, CNC setups or assembly steps.

02 / the system

Two grids. One simple idea.

A 1,200 mm coordination grid sets the main frame. A 600 mm sub-grid gives the smaller moves room to happen.

Main posts are only activated where span, stability and opening rules allow. Wall openings, secondary members and infill follow the finer grid. Overall dimensions are derived from the module count rather than drawn freehand.

Height uses a separate 150 mm step. Change the width or length and the nodes, parts, IDs and bill of materials regenerate from the same underlying rules.

03 / the parts

A small vocabulary.

The library is intentionally made of families, not a huge catalogue of finished products.

A part becomes a specific component only when its material, length, machining and connection are resolved for a real configuration. Until then, the system keeps the definition flexible.

04 / the join
The join is where the system earns its keep.

The first connection family is intentionally plain: let bearing do the obvious work, use mechanical restraint where movement must be controlled, and keep the joint visible enough to inspect.

Five connection types are being developed from the post–beam node through to the foundation interface. Repeated assembly comes before confidence; load testing comes before any structural claim.

05 / testing

Say what is known. Leave room for what isn't.

The public status stays deliberately boring: a configuration is only called validated when the right calculations, tests and approvals exist.

The current work sits at the geometry and early construction-system stages. It is enough to explore rules and make physical test pieces; it is not enough to make project-level structural promises.

06 / what next

Build the boring bits first.

The next useful step is not a configurator. It is proving that the same parts, records and rules survive contact with a workshop and a small test build.

OpenBay 0.1 is an independent R&D baseline for a modular, prefabricated, CNC-manufactured timber kit of parts. The definition is conceptual / pre-engineering and does not replace project-specific engineering or approval.
