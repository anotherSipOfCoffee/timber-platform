System 0.1 · Conceptual / Pre-Engineering

A Parametric Timber Construction Platform

Can a small kit of CNC-cut timber parts generate real architectural variety, while staying simple enough to build, test, and trust?

Independent R&D · Not a product for sale · No structural claims are made

Read the system, in order ↓

01 — The Question

Variety without chaos

Standard construction treats almost every building as a one-off. This project asks whether a small family of standardized, CNC-manufactured timber parts can generate real architectural variety — different sizes, roof types, window layouts — while keeping structure, manufacturing, and assembly predictable enough to actually trust.

The hypothesis only holds if variety grows faster than complexity: more valid building configurations, without a matching explosion in unique parts or assembly steps. A single beautiful prototype isn't evidence. The evidence has to come from many configurations tested against real fabrication data and structural review.

02 — The Grid

Two grids, not one

A 1,200 mm grid places the main structural frame — few enough posts and beams to keep the logic simple. A finer 600 mm grid underneath controls where windows, doors, and interior walls can sit. Every dimension in the system is a multiple of one of these two numbers — nothing is drawn freehand.

Height uses a separate 150 mm vertical increment, so ceiling height isn't trapped by the plan grid. Changing the width or length of a building regenerates every node, part, and label automatically — nothing is moved by hand.

03 — The Kit

A few part families, not a catalogue

A small number of reusable part types — not hundreds of finished, pre-cut SKUs. A part becomes a specific, orderable component only once its material, length, and connections are resolved for a given building. Everything else stays a flexible rule.

Floor, wall, and roof panels attach to a sparse primary frame through defined interface zones and can vary independently — trading a little component minimalism for repairability, façade freedom, and the ability to swap suppliers.

04 — Connections

Bearing carries gravity, hardware carries restraint

The hardest problem in a kit-of-parts system isn't the parts — it's how they meet. The first connection family prioritizes clarity and inspection over hidden, sculptural joinery: gravity is carried by simple bearing, hardware handles restraint, and every joint stays visually checkable after assembly.

Five joint types are currently being defined and tested — from the post-to-beam connection down to the adjustable foundation interface — each validated through repeated assembly trials before it's trusted.

05 — Validation

What can honestly be claimed, right now

No configuration is ever presented as structurally validated until it's actually calculated, tested, and signed off for its jurisdiction. This ladder is public on purpose — it's the honest boundary between what the system currently knows and what still needs an engineer.
